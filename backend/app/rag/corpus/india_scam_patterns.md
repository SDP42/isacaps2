# Digital arrest
A caller poses as CBI, police, customs or narcotics officers on a video call, claims a parcel or bank account in the victim's name is linked to crime, and keeps the victim on camera for hours. Victims are told not to disconnect or tell anyone, then pressured to pay a "security deposit" or "verification amount". No Indian agency conducts arrests over video calls or asks for money to clear a name.

# Family emergency
A message or cloned voice note claims to be a son, daughter, parent or sibling in an accident, in police custody or in hospital. It often comes from a "friend's number" or "new number", asks for money on a UPI ID immediately, and says "kisi ko mat batana" (don't tell anyone). Verification: call the relative on their saved number and ask the family code word.

# CEO / executive fraud
An email, WhatsApp message, voice call or video meeting impersonates a senior leader and asks an employee to wire money to a new vendor, buy gift cards or share data. Hallmarks: the leader "can't take calls", the matter is confidential, approvals should be skipped, and the deadline is hours away.

# KYC expiry and utility disconnection
SMS warns that a bank KYC has expired or electricity will be cut tonight, and gives a link or a number to call. The link harvests credentials, or the caller asks for an OTP or a screen-sharing app.

# Job and reward scams
Offers of easy work-from-home income or lottery winnings that require a registration or processing fee to a UPI ID.

# IT help-desk impersonation
Someone claiming to be IT support asks for an OTP to "finish a reset", or asks the employee to install AnyDesk or TeamViewer. Genuine IT teams never ask for OTPs or remote access codes by chat or phone.
