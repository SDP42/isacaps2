# Key judgement
Synthetic media threats to organisations include executive impersonation for financial fraud, impersonation to gain access to networks and data, and damage to brand reputation. The barrier to producing convincing fakes has fallen sharply.

# Detection and authentication
The guidance recommends combining detection technology with authentication: content provenance standards such as C2PA, digital signatures and watermarking help establish where media came from. Real-time verification capabilities and passive detection should both be considered.

# Planning and response
Organisations should plan for deepfake incidents, rehearse responses, and train staff. Verification should be required for sensitive requests: confirm identity through a known secondary channel, and be suspicious of requests that bypass normal procedures, demand secrecy or create time pressure.

# Signs worth checking
Inconsistent lip movement and audio, unnatural blinking or facial edges, unusual lighting, and audio with odd pacing or tone. Context matters more: a request that does not fit the person's normal behaviour or channel is a strong warning sign.
