# Multi-person video meeting fraud, Hong Kong (2024)
A finance employee at the engineering firm Arup joined a video conference in which the CFO and colleagues all appeared, but every other participant was a deepfake. After initial suspicion of a phishing email, the meeting convinced the employee to make 15 transfers totalling about HK$200 million to five accounts. The fraud was discovered only when the employee later checked with head office. Lesson: video presence is not verification; confirm through an independent channel.

# Voice-cloned CEO, UK energy firm (2019)
The CEO of a UK subsidiary received a call from what sounded like the parent company's chief executive, including his accent and manner, asking for an urgent transfer of about €220,000 to a Hungarian supplier. A second call asking for more money raised suspicion because it came from an unexpected number.

# Executive impersonation attempt, WPP (2024)
Attackers set up a meeting using a cloned voice and public video of the CEO, with a fake messaging account, to solicit money and personal details from an agency leader. The attempt failed because staff were alert.

# Ferrari CEO impersonation attempt (2024)
An executive received WhatsApp messages and a call imitating the CEO's voice and southern Italian accent about a confidential acquisition. He asked a personal question only the real CEO could answer, and the caller hung up. Lesson: a shared secret defeats a clone.
